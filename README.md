# SlopeShield
Hilly and mountainous regions are highly vulnerable to landslides, especially during heavy rainfall. Landslides can suddenly block roads, damage infrastructure, and endanger travellers and emergency responders. Users often have no way of knowing which areas along their route are risky or which alternative roads are safer, and this lack of accessible, real-time risk information can delay evacuation and disaster response.SlopeShield uses historical and geographical data such as rainfall, slope, soil moisture, elevation, previous landslide occurrences, and road conditions to compute a landslide risk score for each location and classify it into Low, Moderate, High, or Critical risk levels. The road network is modelled as a graph, with locations as vertices and roads as weighted edges combining distance and risk. Dijkstra's algorithm computes the safest available route between a source and destination, while a priority queue prioritizes high-risk locations during processing.
Key Technologies like C++, Object-Oriented Programming and core Data Structures (graph, priority queue, vector, and hash table)along with Dijkstra's algorithm, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Leaflet, OpenStreetMap and CSV/JSON datasets are used.
Our proposed outcome includes a functional web-based system that identifies landslide-prone areas, displays their risk levels on an interactive map  and recommends the safest route between two locations while demonstrating the practical application of DSA and OOP to a real world disaster management problem.
